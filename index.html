The honest limit

If shifts at your sites are gone in under ten seconds, no polling design wins reliably and you need an approved push feed instead. If they survive a minute or two, a one-minute poll solves your problem outright.

We measure which, in week one, before the build commits to anything. That measurement is the first deliverable.

What I will not build

  • Nothing that defeats a bot control. No device-fingerprint spoofing, no CAPTCHA solving, no pretending a server is your phone. Scheduler's own privacy declaration says it collects a device ID linked to your identity, so that fight is one you lose slowly and then all at once.
  • No faster-than-told polling. A 429 with Retry-After is honoured exactly.
  • No auto-accept without written permission. Enforced in code.

This is not caution for its own sake. A build that gets your account closed has negative value, and you cannot appeal your way back into a week's work.

The request that costs nothing

Ten minutes, and its answer decides the shape of the build. Full text is in asset 02, ready to send.

Clause 3.3 of Randstad's terms says automated searches and intelligent
agents need prior approval, so I am asking for that rather than assuming it.

 1. Is there an approved way to get faster notification of shifts released
    to me, such as an alerts setting, an email feed or an API?
 2. If not, would you permit a read-only check of my own account at a
    stated rate, in writing?
 3. Is Scheduler being replaced by the Randstad UK: Jobs For You app?

Three outcomes, all useful. Yes in writing means the fast build is safe. A pointer to a setting may solve it for nothing. No means you keep notify-and-confirm, which stays inside normal app use.

Two near-misses I chased and ruled out, so nobody repeats the work. connect.randstad.com looked right because the app's bundle id is uk.co.randstad.connect — it is Happeo, Randstad's staff intranet, posting to login.happeo.com/saml. And timesheets.randstad.co.uk is InTime.

So browser automation is off the table, and that is one of the five skills your post asks for. Any bid offering Selenium against "the web version" has not checked whether one exists.

The newer one is published by Swipejobs, a third-party workforce platform, not by Randstad. Its Play listing says the product is "rapidly expanding across the US", so it is a platform Randstad has adopted.

The open question, and I want to be straight that it is open. I found no announcement from either company about migration or a retirement date. It is reasonable to wonder whether Scheduler is being replaced. It is not reasonable for me to tell you it is. Building against an app you may not be on next year is a real risk, and your consultant can answer it in one question.

3. Your own evidence

Scheduler sits at 1.8 stars. A public review of the Android build says alerts arrive too late and the work is gone before the worker sees it. Version 1.15's release note reads, in full: "In addition to some bug fixes and improvements, the app now allows for new login experiences for new users."

You are not trying to game anything. You are trying to be told about your own available work quickly enough to answer. That framing matters, because it is the one a permission request can actually succeed with.
